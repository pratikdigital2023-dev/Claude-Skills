# Project Proposal Writer

Transforms a discovery conversation into a tight, defensible proposal that
clients can sign. Optimized for fixed-fee web/marketing-site engagements in
the $20K–$150K range.

## When to use

- A prospect asked "what would this cost?"
- You need a one-page summary plus a deeper SoW
- You're protecting yourself against scope creep before kickoff

## Inputs to gather

Run discovery before writing. Don't skip these — vague proposals lose deals
or eat margin:

1. **Business goal**: revenue target, lead-gen target, brand-launch date
2. **Audience**: 1–3 personas, primary action per persona
3. **Pages**: rough sitemap with page count
4. **Content**: who writes copy, who provides photography
5. **Tech constraints**: existing CMS, marketing stack, hosting preferences
6. **Integrations**: CRM, ESP, payment, analytics, ATS, etc.
7. **Brand state**: existing identity vs. needs rebrand
8. **Timeline**: launch deadline + reason for it
9. **Budget range**: tier, not number ("under $30K", "$50–80K", etc.)
10. **Decision process**: who signs, how many rounds of review

## Proposal structure

```
1. One-paragraph framing of the client's goal (in their words)
2. Recommended approach (3–5 sentences)
3. Scope of work (deliverables, by phase)
4. Out of scope (explicit list — protects margin)
5. Assumptions (content delivery, review windows, decision-maker availability)
6. Timeline (Gantt-style with milestones)
7. Investment (fixed fee, broken into milestone payments)
8. Change order policy (hourly rate for OOS work)
9. Terms (IP transfer on final payment, kill fee, warranty period)
```

## Phase template (web build)

| Phase | Duration | Deliverables | Payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery & strategy | 1–2 wk | Sitemap, IA, brand audit | 25% kickoff |
| Design | 3–5 wk | Wireframes → hi-fi → approved | 25% on design approval |
| Build | 4–8 wk | Staging site, CMS, integrations | 25% on staging |
| QA & launch | 1–2 wk | UAT pass, go-live, training | 25% on launch |

## Pricing rules

- Fixed fee, not hourly. Hourly invites negotiation; fixed forces clarity.
- Build in 15–20% buffer for the "unknown unknowns" phase (usually content
  migration or integration weirdness).
- Stage payments to keep AR < 1 phase of work outstanding.
- Always quote a **kill fee** of 50% if cancelled after design approval.

## Out-of-scope examples (use these — they save fights)

- Copywriting beyond X pages
- Stock photography sourcing
- Translation / localization
- A/B test setup beyond initial GA4 install
- Email template design
- Ongoing SEO content production
- Hosting fees (passed through at cost)

## Red flags during discovery

- "We just need a quick site" + 40-page sitemap
- No single decision-maker
- "Match this competitor" without budget context
- Brand-new business with no audience research
- Fixed launch date driven by external event with no flexibility

When you spot these, raise them in the proposal as **assumptions** that, if
violated, trigger a change order.

## Output artifacts

Always deliver:
1. **One-pager** (executive summary, cost, timeline) — for the buyer's boss
2. **Full SoW** (10–15 pages) — for the contract
3. **MSA + SoW combo** if the client is enterprise

## Quick-fire prompts

- "Draft a 1-page executive summary for [project]"
- "Build the milestone payment schedule for a $[X] engagement"
- "List 15 out-of-scope items for a marketing website redesign"
- "Write the assumptions section for a project where the client is providing
   their own copy"
